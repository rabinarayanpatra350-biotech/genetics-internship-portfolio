This Week 3 report presents a complete phylogenetic analysis and tree reconstruction using publicly available genetic sequence data, covering every objective of the task. I retrieved cytochrome c oxidase subunit 1 (COX1) sequences for twelve vertebrate species — five mammals, two birds, a reptile, an amphibian, two ray-finned fish and a jawless fish outgroup — from the UniProt and NCBI public databases, and I document the full methodology: sequence retrieval, reference-identity screening, multiple sequence alignment with FAMSA, distance estimation under p-distance, Poisson and BLOSUM62/Kimura models, tree reconstruction by Neighbour-Joining and UPGMA, and node support from 500 bootstrap replicates. The analysis recovers the primate, mammal, bird and ray-finned fish clades with strong support (100%, 94%, 100% and 99%), and the pairwise divergences match published values almost exactly (human–chimpanzee 98.8% identity, human–mouse 90.8%, human–chicken 84.6%, chicken–pigeon 96.3%). Rather than presenting a misleading tree, the report diagnoses why the deepest nodes are not resolved by any of seven substitution models tested — long-branch attraction with sparse taxon sampling, single-gene data, and an anomalously conserved outgroup record. It also documents a concrete data-quality failure: a GenBank record submitted as chicken COX1 proved 99.8% identical to human, so screening is treated as a required analytical step. The report closes with evolutionary interpretation, limitations and eight specific recommendations for future studies, supported by eight figures, five tables and twenty-four references.
