This Week 1 report of my Genetics Assistant internship presents a complete bioinformatics analysis of a simulated genetic sequence dataset, covering every objective of the task with fully reproducible methods. I designed a 1,500 bp simulated gene, SIMG1, with a defined architecture (5' UTR, a 1,299 bp coding sequence carrying a glycine-rich loop and a catalytic DxxxxN motif, and a 3' UTR with a polyadenylation signal) and derived twelve sample sequences representing six cases and six controls, introducing a documented catalogue of substitutions, in-frame deletions, two premature-stop variants and a frameshift from a fixed random seed. The dataset is self-verifying: the simulation script asserts every designed effect after construction. The analysis pipeline, implemented in Python with Biopython, FAMSA and SciPy, runs six documented steps: quality control and six-frame ORF detection; global Needleman-Wunsch alignment with exact variant calling (81 calls at 13 sites) and functional annotation; BLAST-style seed-and-extend homology search against a four-sequence database; multiple sequence alignment with conservation profiling and an enrichment test; substitution spectrum analysis (transition/transversion ratio 2.36); and Fisher exact association testing with Bonferroni correction. One variant, the missense substitution g.612C>G (P171R), is carried by all six cases and no controls (p = 0.0022) and survives correction, making it the leading candidate marker, while three loss-of-function variants truncate the protein. The report includes annotated screenshots of the analysis process, a summary of key insights, honest limitations, and twelve references.
