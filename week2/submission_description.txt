This Week 2 report of my Genetics Assistant internship presents a complete experimental design simulation for gene expression analysis, covering every objective of the task. It opens with a literature review of expression-measurement methodologies — qRT-PCR, microarrays, bulk RNA sequencing, single-cell and spatial transcriptomics and multi-omic designs — and of how they are used in evolutionary and disease research, then justifies the chosen approach: a controlled RNA-seq study with orthogonal qRT-PCR validation. The designed experiment models hypoxia and reoxygenation injury in human endothelial cells across four arms (normoxia, hypoxia, hypoxia with reoxygenation, and hypoxia with HIF1A knockdown) of six biological replicates each, with four categories of control, an explicit variable structure, and a sample size justified by a negative-binomial power analysis computed in the accompanying code: at n = 6 the design reaches 89% power for a two-fold change at moderate biological variability and essentially complete power for four-fold changes, while the smallest reliably detectable effect is 1.83-fold. The report specifies the full step-by-step procedure from culture and quality gates through sequencing to bioinformatics, includes a programmatically generated randomisation plan verified to be unconfounded across batch, passage and operator, states quantitative and falsifiable predictions (including the counter-intuitive suppression of HIF1A mRNA during hypoxia), defines five independent validation lines with acceptance thresholds, and closes with a twelve-row troubleshooting table, honest limitations, feasibility assessment and twenty references.
